/**
 * Dun & Bradstreet Registered Seal — the embed's sandbox policy.
 *
 * ▶ THE MECHANISM, READ FROM THE LIVE SEAL PAGE (2026-09-04). Not inferred —
 * `profiles.dunsregistered.com/TpSeal.aspx` (which the `src` below 302s to)
 * contains:
 *
 *     function toSubmit() {
 *       var form2 = document.getElementById('form2');
 *       form2.action = 'https://profiles.dunsregistered.com/TPSING-COMP-004.aspx';
 *       window.open('', 'formresult', 'width=1020,height=710,...');
 *       form2.submit();
 *     }
 *     <form id="form2" method="post" action="" target="formresult">
 *       <input name="iSealKey" type="hidden" value="…" />
 *       <a id="ATpSeal" onclick="toSubmit();return false;" …>
 *
 * So a click opens an EMPTY NAMED WINDOW and then **POSTs a form into it**,
 * carrying `iSealKey`. That single fact determines every token below.
 *
 * ▶ WHAT WENT WRONG, TWICE, AND WHY THE TOKENS ARE WHAT THEY ARE.
 *   2026-06-20  A security review hardened this to
 *               `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin"`. No `allow-popups`
 *               → `window.open` blocked → the click did nothing at all, for
 *               eleven weeks. Nothing asserted anything about it.
 *   2026-09-04  Fixed by adding popups — but WITHOUT `allow-forms`, so the
 *               window opened and stayed on `about:blank`. Chromium: "Blocked
 *               form submission … because the form's frame is sandboxed and the
 *               'allow-forms' permission is not set." A blank tab instead of a
 *               dead click: a different symptom, the same root cause — a token
 *               the badge needs was missing.
 *
 * ▶ EVERY TOKEN, EACH TIED TO A LINE OF THEIR CODE:
 *   allow-scripts                   `toSubmit()` is JavaScript.
 *   allow-same-origin               the page is a real origin; the form posts
 *                                   to its own host.
 *   allow-popups                    `window.open('', 'formresult', …)`.
 *   allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox  without it the opened profile inherits this
 *                                   sandbox and is itself crippled — a blank
 *                                   tab traded for a broken one.
 *   allow-forms                     `form2.submit()`. THIS is what fills the
 *                                   window; without it the popup never leaves
 *                                   about:blank.
 *
 * ▶ WHAT STAYS DENIED, and note this list got SHORTER on evidence, not longer:
 * `allow-modals`, `allow-downloads`, `allow-pointer-lock`, and **both** forms of
 * top-navigation. `allow-top-navigation-by-user-activation` was granted on
 * 2026-09-04 by reasoning that the seal *might* use `target="_top"`. Reading the
 * page shows it does not: the anchor carries `onclick="…return false;"` and has
 * no `href`, so it never navigates the parent. Removed — a grant nothing needs
 * is a grant that should not exist.
 *
 * ⚠️ CLICKJACKING IS NOT GOVERNED BY THIS ATTRIBUTE. The 2026-06-20 rationale
 * cited it; that is our own `X-Frame-Options` / `frame-ancestors`, which control
 * who may frame US and are untouched by anything here. Only the frame-busting
 * half of that reasoning was ever about this sandbox.
 *
 * ▶ METHOD NOTE, because this cost two rounds. The seal HAS a known-good
 * configuration in git — commits `d5fa61ab6` (2026-06-17) and `61430ce0c`
 * (2026-06-18) carry NO sandbox and NO referrerPolicy, and it worked. The right
 * first move on "this used to work" is to read that commit and diff, not to
 * reason from the sandbox spec. Both regressions came from adding restrictions
 * without checking what the third party actually does.
 */
